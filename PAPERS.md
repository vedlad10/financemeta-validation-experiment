# Three related papers / benchmarks

1. **Cerqueira, Torgo, and Mozetič (2020), “Evaluating time series forecasting models: an empirical study on performance estimation methods.”** *Machine Learning* 109, 1997–2028. DOI: [10.1007/s10994-020-05910-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05910-7); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11744). This is the closest protocol reference: it compares cross-validation and out-of-sample estimators on synthetic and 62 real-world series, and finds that order-preserving out-of-sample methods are strongest when non-stationarity is present. It also documents gap-based `hv`-blocked validation, the general idea behind purging adjacent dependent rows.

2. **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, and Zhu (2016), “The Probability of Backtest Overfitting.”** *Journal of Computational Finance* 20(4), 39–69. DOI: [10.21314/JCF.2016.322](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2016.322); [author manuscript](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf). This paper formalizes backtest overfitting as selecting an in-sample winner that performs below the median out of sample. It motivates freezing the trial set, reporting performance degradation, and retaining unfavorable out-of-sample results rather than tuning around them.

3. **Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020), “Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning.”** *Review of Financial Studies* 33(5), 2223–2273. DOI: [10.1093/rfs/hhaa009](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa009); [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398). This is a widely used equity-return ML benchmark. Its strictly time-ordered training/validation/testing design and out-of-sample \(R^2\) reporting motivate the lockbox and zero-return benchmark used here; the present experiment intentionally reduces its broad cross-sectional setup to one equity and one linear model so the validation protocol is the only moving part.

These sources do not prove in advance that shuffled validation will look better for AAPL. They justify the risk being tested and the conservative evaluation design; the experiment is allowed to falsify the hypothesis.
